Why Young People Must Be at the Centre of Africa's Food Systems Transformation

Resilience for Africa's Future (RAFO)

Africa's food systems sit at the heart of some of the continent's most important challenges and opportunities. How food is produced, distributed and consumed affects livelihoods, communities, the environment and our ability to respond to a changing climate.

For Resilience for Africa's Future (RAFO), one principle is fundamental: young people must be part of shaping what comes next.

More Than Beneficiaries

Young people are often discussed as beneficiaries of development programmes. RAFO believes we need to go further.

Young people can be farmers, agripreneurs, innovators, researchers, extension professionals, community leaders and problem-solvers. They can bring new ideas into agriculture while learning from the knowledge and experience already present within farming communities.

Putting young people at the centre therefore does not mean replacing farmers or disregarding generations of agricultural knowledge. It means creating opportunities for generations to work together.

Agriculture Is More Than Farming

The future of agriculture is not limited to working directly on a farm.

Food systems create opportunities across production, processing, technology, data, extension, logistics, marketing and other parts of agricultural value chains.

This wider perspective matters because young people have different skills and aspirations. Some may want to produce food. Others may develop technologies, provide services, analyse agricultural data, build enterprises or help connect farmers to markets and information.

A resilient food system needs this diversity.

Technology Must Solve Real Problems

Digital tools, precision agriculture, data and smart technologies can contribute to agricultural transformation, but technology should not become an end in itself.

Innovation is most valuable when it responds to real challenges experienced by farmers and communities.

This is why RAFO is interested in practical approaches that connect technology with agriculture, climate resilience and local needs. Young people can play an important role in translating new tools into solutions that are useful and accessible.

Climate Resilience Is Part of the Food-System Conversation

Food systems cannot be separated from climate change.

Farmers and communities are making decisions in the context of changing environmental conditions. Building resilience therefore requires knowledge, adaptation, sustainable agricultural practices and stronger connections between people, landscapes and food production.

Young people should be equipped not only to participate in agriculture, but also to help shape approaches that are environmentally responsible and responsive to climate realities.

Building Pathways, Not Just Training

Skills development matters, but training alone is not enough.

Young people also need pathways to apply what they learn: opportunities to innovate, build enterprises, support farmers, participate in value chains and contribute to their communities.

For RAFO, this is an important part of the challenge ahead. Our ambition is to help connect learning with meaningful participation in sustainable agri-food systems.

A Future Built Together

Africa's food-system transformation will require collaboration between young people, farmers, communities, public institutions, researchers, civil society and responsible private-sector actors.

No single organisation or generation can build resilient food systems alone.

RAFO's vision is of a food-secure and resilient Africa where young people have the knowledge, opportunities and confidence to contribute meaningfully to sustainable agriculture, healthy landscapes and climate-resilient communities.

Young people are not simply beneficiaries of Africa's future.

They are partners in building it.
